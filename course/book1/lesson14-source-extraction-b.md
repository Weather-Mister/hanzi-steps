## Grammar I — Time-Duration after verbal 了

- **B1L14-G001**
- Source: printed pp. 306–307 / PDF pp. 333–334.
- Function: indicates the duration of a **completed activity**.

Core source examples:
- 媽媽的朋友在臺北玩了三天。
- 老師在美國住了一年。
- 李小姐在語言中心工作了一個月。

Structural requirements:
1. The taught construction is verbal 了 followed by a time duration.
2. If a transitive verb is followed by its object, the verb is repeated:
   - 他租房子租了半年。
   - 他住臺北住了三年。
3. If the object/location is fronted or otherwise moved away from the post-verbal object position, repetition is not needed:
   - 房子他只租了半年。
   - 臺北他只住了三年。

Questions/drills cover:
- 嗎;
- 是不是;
- 多久;
- 幾個月 / 幾個鐘頭;
- repeated-verb questions such as 打網球打了幾個鐘頭.

This grammar must remain distinct from G002: G001 is explicitly framed as completed duration.

## Grammar II — Completion-to-date with Double 了

- **B1L14-G002**
- Source: printed pp. 307–308 / PDF pp. 334–335.
- Function: duration accumulated up to the time of speaking.
- The textbook explicitly states that the action **may or may not continue**, depending on context.

Core examples:
- 她已經在臺灣玩了一年了。
- 陳小姐在美國住了五年了。
- 我工作了兩個月了。
- 這間房子，他已經租了半年了。
- 他們學中文學了三個星期了。

The drill repeatedly asks/answers **多久了** and uses **已經** where contextually relevant.

Required contrast:
- **G001** = completed duration.
- **G002** = duration accumulated through the speech point; continuation is contextual.

## Grammar III — 快／要／快要 … 了 “about to”

- **B1L14-G003**
- Source: printed pp. 308–309 / PDF pp. 335–336.
- Function: sentence-final 了 combines with 快, 要, or 快要 to mark an imminent event/change.

Core examples:
- 快下雨了。
- 電影要結束了。
- 爸爸快要到家了。

Questions include:
- 你媽媽的生日快到了嗎？
- 比賽要開始了嗎？
- 哥哥的女朋友快要回法國了嗎？

Usage restriction:
- In Taiwan, the textbook says the disyllabic **快要** is preferred.
- With an explicit time word, **快要 cannot be used**; the source marks *他明天快要回來了 as unacceptable.

The following drill requires choosing an imminent-event response from contextual evidence such as a date, the current time, an arrival estimate, or strong wind. This target is **imminence**, not generic future reference.

## Grammar IV — Comparison 更 “even more so”

- **B1L14-G004**
- Source: printed pp. 309–310 / PDF pp. 336–337.
- Function: 更 presents a degree superior to a fact/baseline already stated or understood.
- 更 is an adverb and precedes a state verb.
- This lesson specifically teaches it inside 比-comparisons.

Core examples:
- 他很高，他哥哥比他更高。
- 今年比去年更冷。
- 我覺得芒果比西瓜更好吃。

Questions:
- 這次的颱風是不是比上次的更大？
- 在學校上網是不是比在家裡更快？

The source says **是不是** is typical in this structure.

The drill compares prices, quantities, study duration, and travel time. The “even” baseline/presupposition is part of the target and must not be flattened to plain “more.”

## Grammar V — Inferior Comparison 沒有

- **B1L14-G005**
- Source: printed pp. 310–314 / PDF pp. 337–341.
- Core form: **A 沒有 B 那麼／這麼 + State Verb**.
- Function: A is not as [property] as B.

Representative source examples:
- 哥哥沒有爸爸那麼高。
- 火車沒有高鐵那麼快。
- 我的中文沒有老師的那麼好。
- 臭豆腐沒有牛肉麵這麼好吃。
- 甜點沒有小籠包那麼好做。
- 這次的颱風沒有上次的那麼可怕。

Explicit source notes:
1. The pattern is normally negative with 沒（有）.
2. 那麼 / 這麼 may sometimes be omitted.
3. Positive **A 有 B 那麼／這麼 + State Verb** is rare except in questions.
4. Questions use an A-not-A **有沒有** pattern.
5. A question such as 今天有沒有昨天熱？ presupposes that yesterday was fairly hot.

The source explicitly contrasts:
- equal degree: **A 跟 B 一樣 + State Verb**
- inferior degree: **A 沒有 B 那麼 + State Verb**
- superior degree: **A 比 B + State Verb**

## Pragmatics and source usage

- **B1L14-P001** — **更** carries a baseline implication: the comparison is “even more,” not simply a neutral comparative.
- **B1L14-P002** — **有沒有 B 那麼…?** comparison questions can presuppose that B has a salient/high degree of the property; the textbook makes this explicit with 今天有沒有昨天熱？
- **B1L14-P003** — **吧** in 開始下雪了吧？ presents a speaker assumption/inference and invites confirmation.
- **B1L14-P004** — **呢** in 妳怎麼沒帶傘呢？ is part of a contextual why-question expressing immediate concern/surprise rather than a neutral information request.
- **B1L14-P005** — **慢走**, especially in 小心慢走, is a leave-taking/care formula rather than a literal command to “walk slowly.”
- **B1L14-P006** — the textbook explicitly identifies **快要** as the Taiwan-preferred disyllabic form in the imminence construction.

## Culture — Typhoon Days-Off

- **B1L14-CUL001**
- Source: printed p. 320 / PDF p. 347.
- The source explains “typhoon days” as school/work suspensions associated with severe typhoon conditions.
- The page gives source-era thresholds: average wind speeds of class 7 or higher or gusts of class 10 or higher expected to pass through an area within the next four hours.
- It states that school/work are cancelled until the storm passes.
- It describes a source-era administrative history in which the Directorate-General of Personnel Administration received decision authority in 1993, used Central Weather Bureau forecasts and local-government suggestions, and later no longer decided for the entire nation because decisions were vested in regional governments.
- It tells learners to consult local news or then-current official/contact information for suspension updates.

This is authoritative as textbook source coverage. It must **not** be silently presented as current Taiwanese administrative policy without a separate present-day verification step.
