## Grammar I — 一…就… “as soon as…”

Source function:
- A and B are sequential events;
- B happens immediately after A.

Source structural requirements:
- 一 precedes Event A;
- 就 precedes Event B;
- both markers come after the subject;
- when the subject is the same in both clauses, the repeated subject may be omitted;
- the two events may be affirmative or negative.

Source question restriction:
- ordinary A-not-A is not available for this construction;
- the textbook uses 嗎 or 是不是 to form questions.

Representative source patterns include:
- 我一下課，就回來。
- 他一回國，就找工作。
- 我妹妹一回去，就給媽媽打電話。
- 我一下課，就去吃晚飯。
- 老闆今天早上一到公司，就不開心。

Phase 2 must teach more than the English gloss “as soon as”; the ordering, subject placement/omission, polarity, and question restriction are part of the source grammar.

## Grammar II — completed action with verbal 了

Source function:
- 了 is placed after the verb to indicate that an action/event has been completed or has taken place.

The textbook explicitly contrasts a completed event with an intended/future event, e.g. 買了… vs. 要買….

Source negation:
- use 沒 before the verb;
- verbal 了 does **not** appear in the negative.

The textbook explicitly marks:
- *我沒吃了晚飯。 as wrong;
- 我沒吃晚飯。 as correct.

Source question form:
- completed-action yes/no questions may place 沒有 at the end, e.g. 吃了午餐沒有？ / 付了沒有？ / 開始了沒有？

This grammar must not be reduced to “了 = past tense”, and it must be explicitly distinguished from already-known sentence-final 了.

## Grammar III — 不 negation vs. 沒（有） negation

The textbook organizes this by verb type.

### Action verbs

Both markers are possible with different meanings:
- 不 → habitual non-action;
- 不 → intention not to do something;
- 沒（有） → an action did not happen in the past.

### State verbs

- use 不 to indicate the contrary state;
- the source does not use 沒 for this category.

### Process verbs

- use 沒（有） to indicate non-happening;
- the source does not use 不 for this category.

Taiwan-specific usage note:
- the textbook states that in Taiwan 沒有 + Verb is more common than bare 沒 + Verb;
- it contrasts this with greater use of bare 沒 + Verb in mainland China.

Phase 2 must preserve this Taiwan-oriented usage note because Hanzi Steps targets Taiwan Mandarin.

## Grammar IV — all-inclusive question words with 都 / 也

The textbook uses question words in declarative sentences to express total inclusion or total exclusion.

Affirmative system:
- 誰都… → everyone
- 哪裡都… → everywhere
- 什麼都… → everything
- 什麼時候都… → anytime / all the time
- 怎麼 + V + 都… → whichever way / no matter how

Negative system:
- question word + 都 or 也 + 不 / 沒… expresses total exclusion;
- the negative marker follows 都 / 也.

Source examples cover:
- 誰也不…
- 哪裡都沒…
- 什麼也不…
- 什麼時候都不…
- 怎麼做都不好吃

Source question restriction:
- the textbook states that this pattern forms questions with 嗎.

This is a larger system than the already-known ordinary 都 “all/both” and must be taught as such.

## Grammar V — 多 / 少 + Verb

Source function:
- pre-verbal 多 means do more than planned/usual;
- pre-verbal 少 means do less/fewer than planned/usual.

Source usage:
- can refer to past or future events;
- 一點 often modifies the object.

Representative source uses include:
- 少買東西
- 多看書，少看電視
- 多穿衣服
- 多學一點中文
- 多做了一點菜
- 少做一點功課

This must be distinguished from earlier quantity uses of 多 / 少.

## Grammar VI — 是不是 confirmation questions

Source function:
- seeks confirmation of information already known or obvious from context;
- it is not simply a neutral request for new information.

Source structure:
- Subject + 是不是 + VP?

The textbook explicitly contrasts 是不是 questions with 嗎 and ordinary A-not-A questions.

The source also shows environments where ordinary A-not-A is not available but 是不是 (or, where stated, 嗎) can be used, including:
- 比 comparison;
- 太…了.

Phase 2 must preserve the pragmatic “confirmation” function, not teach 是不是 as a mechanically interchangeable yes/no marker.

## Grammar VII — comparison with 跟…一樣

Source function:
- compares two people/things and says they are the same/equal;
- an explicit shared quality can follow 一樣.

Core pattern:
- A 跟 B 一樣 (+ state verb / property)

Source extensions:
- identical objects: 你點的菜跟我點的一樣。
- equal degree: 我跟我妹妹一樣高。
- equal preference/activity: 我跟我朋友一樣喜歡看電視。

Negation has two distinct scopes:
1. A 跟 B **不一樣** … → A and B differ in the relevant quality.
2. A **不跟 B 一樣** … → B is specifically not the comparison target; another target may be supplied.

Questions:
- 一樣不一樣…？
- 是不是…一樣…？

The textbook includes both simple sameness and equality of a property. Phase 2 must preserve the scope distinction in negation and not collapse all negative forms into “different”.

## Integrated activities and what they require

### Activity I — Going to Night Markets

Source goal: **Learning to ask people about their preferences.**

The task operationalizes that goal through completed / non-completed actions at a night market. The source models:
- 吃了臭豆腐沒有？
- 沒有，我點了小籠包。
- 吃了，我吃了臭豆腐。
- 我沒吃臭豆腐。

This directly reinforces Grammar II and III rather than functioning as optional vocabulary decoration.

### Activity II — How Do You Celebrate Your Birthday?

Goal: briefly compare cultures.

The task asks learners to compare birthday practices such as:
- celebrating with family/friends;
- at home vs. in a restaurant;
- cake;
- gifts;
- buying a cake for friends;
- 豬腳麵線;
- differences from one's home country.

This is direct preparation for the lesson's “compare cultures” outcome and Grammar VII.

### Activity III — Calling a Friend

Goal: make an appointment over the phone.

Learners must discuss:
- why to invite the teacher to a meal;
- when to go;
- where to go;
- how everyone will meet.

The final curriculum must include productive phone/scheduling transfer, not only recognition of Dialogue I.

### Activity IV — Congratulatory Words

Goal: convey wishes on happy occasions.

The task is to write a birthday card, reinforcing the lesson's fixed wishes and appropriate social response.

## Cultural notes

The textbook adds two cultural readings:

1. Congratulatory expressions in Chinese:
   - four-character auspicious formulas are common;
   - 萬事如意、心想事成 are presented as broadly useful wishes.

2. Taiwanese birthdays:
   - traditional lunar-calendar birthday practice vs. common modern Gregorian-calendar practice;
   - importance of one-month and one-year infant celebrations;
   - significance of major decades such as 60, 70, 80;
   - 抓週 as a first-birthday tradition.

These are source-supported cultural notes. They are **not automatically formal Vocabulary I/II or Grammar I–VII targets**. Phase 2 may include concise cultural context where it supports the learning objectives, but must not inflate the canonical vocabulary list with culture-reading words unless deliberately justified.

## Phase 2 dependency map

Phase 2 should respect the following source-driven ordering constraints:

1. Dialogue I can introduce 忘了 for comprehension before verbal 了 is formally generalized.
2. Dialogue I's 一下課，就… provides the natural entry point for Grammar I.
3. Dialogue II introduces 訂了 and completed actions before Grammar II.
4. Grammar II should precede or tightly pair with Grammar III because its negative form directly depends on 沒 without verbal 了.
5. Dialogue II previews 我什麼都吃 and 多吃一點 before Grammar IV/V; productive testing should wait until those systems are taught.
6. Dialogue II also previews 是不是 and 跟…一樣; these can remain comprehension-first until Grammar VI/VII.
7. 一樣 is lexically useful before the full comparison grammar.
8. 祝 + person + wish is productive language, while 萬事如意 / 心想事成 remain fixed congratulatory expressions.
9. No future-new character may appear in learner-facing assessment before its teaching point.
10. Proper-name characters and source 妳 must not silently create new handwriting obligations.
11. 過 vocabulary ownership must be repaired across Book 1 and the existing Book 2 Unit 3 before the Lesson 13 sequence is considered technically clean. The character 過 itself is already a Book 1 character from Unit 33 through 不過, so this is a lexical/sense ownership issue rather than a new-character issue.
